## explain_bar_term {"term":"what is a dry shake?"}

ok in 5 ms; structuredContent 1823 bytes; _meta keys none

```text
Dry shake (technique): https://fixmeadrinkapp.com/glossary/dry-shake
A dry shake is shaking a cocktail without ice first, so egg white or aquafaba whips into a thick, silky foam.
Dry shake: 15 to 20 seconds, no ice
Wet shake after: 10 to 15 seconds with ice
Egg white per drink: about 1 oz (30 ml), one egg white
Aquafaba swap: about 1 oz (30 ml) per egg white
More: egg white and aquafaba need air to turn into foam, and ice gets in the way. Shaking without ice first lets the proteins stretch and trap bubbles. Then you add ice and shake again to chill and dilute the drink. You will see the step in recipes for a sour, a fizz or a flip, usually written as "dry shake, then shake with ice".
How to dry shake a cocktail
1. Add the spirit, citrus, syrup and 1 oz (30 ml) of egg white or aquafaba to the shaker, with no ice.
2. Seal it well and shake hard for 15 to 20 seconds. Pressure builds without ice, so keep a hand on the top.
3. Open it, fill with ice, and shake again for 10 to 15 seconds until the tin feels very cold.
4. Double strain into a chilled glass so the foam comes out smooth and any bits of egg stay behind.
5. Use fresh or pasteurized eggs, and skip raw egg for children, pregnant guests, older adults and anyone with a weak immune system.
```

Say it: koop
Also called: Champagne coupe, Champagne saucer
More: Coupe is French for cup, and it is pronounced koop. The glass started out as a Champagne glass, which is why you will also see it called a champagne coupe or a champagne saucer. Bartenders use it for Champagne and for cocktails.
How to serve a drink in a coupe
1. Chill the coupe well before you pour, using ice water or the freezer.
2. Hold the coupe by its stem, so your hand does not warm the drink.
